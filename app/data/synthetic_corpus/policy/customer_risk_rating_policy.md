# Customer Risk Rating Policy

**Document type:** Policy
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — illustrative only, not real compliance guidance.

## Purpose

This policy describes how customers are assigned an initial risk rating
at onboarding and how that rating is maintained over the life of the
relationship, driving the intensity of ongoing monitoring and periodic
review.

## Rating Factors

Risk ratings consider: customer type (individual vs. business, and
business structure complexity), geography (home jurisdiction and
jurisdictions of significant activity), expected transaction volume and
pattern, industry or occupation, delivery channel (in-person vs. fully
remote onboarding), and any prior adverse history such as past
escalations or closed investigations. No single factor determines the
rating; the combination is weighed holistically by a documented scoring
methodology.

## Rating Tiers

Customers are assigned to one of three tiers: standard, elevated, or
high-risk. High-risk customers include those in higher-risk industries
(e.g., cash-intensive businesses, money services businesses), those with
material ties to higher-risk jurisdictions, and those flagged through
enhanced due diligence as requiring closer attention. Elevated-risk
customers sit between standard monitoring and full enhanced due
diligence.

## Impact on Monitoring

A customer's tier directly sets the monitoring cadence and threshold
sensitivity applied to their account: high-risk customers receive the
most frequent periodic review and the most sensitive transaction
monitoring thresholds, standard customers the least. Ratings are not a
one-time label — they are recalculated whenever a triggering event
occurs, such as a significant and unexplained change in transaction
behavior.

## Review Cadence

Absent a triggering event, ratings are formally reviewed on the schedule
tied to their tier: high-risk annually, elevated-risk every two years, and
standard every three years, consistent with the KYC Policy Summary's
review cadence for identity information.

## Governance

Changes to the scoring methodology itself, as opposed to an individual
customer's score, require sign-off from compliance leadership and are
documented with the rationale for the change.
