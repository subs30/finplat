# Recordkeeping & Retention Policy

**Document type:** Policy
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — illustrative only, not real compliance guidance.

## Purpose

This policy sets minimum retention periods and handling standards for
records generated by KYC, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring,
and suspicious activity reporting processes.

## What Must Be Retained

Retained records include: identity verification documents and the
results of verification checks, beneficial ownership documentation,
sanctions and watchlist screening results (including cleared potential
matches, not only true matches), customer risk rating history and the
basis for each rating change, transaction monitoring alerts and their
disposition, suspicious activity investigation notes, and copies of any
reports filed with authorities along with the underlying supporting
documentation.

## Retention Period

Unless a longer period is required by applicable law or regulation in a
given jurisdiction, records are retained for a minimum of five years
from the later of account closure or the date the record was created.
Records tied to an open investigation or an active report are retained
until the matter is fully resolved, even if that extends beyond the
standard period.

## Format and Accessibility

Records may be retained in electronic form provided they remain
accurate, complete, and readily retrievable in a timely manner upon
request from an authorized reviewer or regulator. Retained records must
not be altered after creation except through a documented correction
process that preserves the original entry.

## Access Controls

Access to retained compliance records is limited to personnel with a
legitimate need, consistent with confidentiality requirements — in
particular, records related to a suspicious activity report are subject
to the same tipping-off restrictions described in the SAR Policy.

## Disposal

At the end of the applicable retention period, records are disposed of
through a controlled process that prevents unauthorized recovery, and the
disposal itself is logged.
