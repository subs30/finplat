# Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR) Policy

**Document type:** Policy
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — illustrative only, not real compliance guidance.

## Purpose

This policy describes how employees identify, escalate, investigate, and
where warranted, report suspicious activity observed in customer accounts
or transactions.

## What Counts as Suspicious

Activity may warrant a suspicious activity report when it has no apparent
lawful purpose, is inconsistent with the customer's known business or
personal profile, or appears structured to avoid reporting or detection
thresholds. Common triggers include: transactions just under reporting
thresholds repeated over a short period, sudden and unexplained changes
in transaction volume or pattern, funds moved rapidly through an account
with little or no legitimate business rationale, and use of accounts by
parties who are not the account holder without adequate explanation.

## Escalation Path

Any employee who observes potentially suspicious activity must escalate
it to the compliance team promptly, using the internal case-referral
process. Employees should not discuss a pending escalation with the
customer or disclose that a review is underway.

## Investigation

Upon escalation, a compliance analyst reviews the account history,
related parties, and transaction patterns to determine whether the
activity has a reasonable explanation. Investigations should be
documented with the specific facts considered and the reasoning behind
the conclusion, whether or not a report is ultimately filed.

## Filing and Timeliness

Where an investigation substantiates suspicion that cannot be reasonably
explained, a report is prepared and filed with the appropriate authority
within the timeframe required by applicable regulation. Internal
service-level targets call for investigations to be completed within 30
days of escalation, with extensions logged and justified when complexity
requires more time.

## Confidentiality

The fact that a report has been filed, or is being considered, is
strictly confidential. It must not be disclosed to the subject of the
report or to any party outside the defined need-to-know group, consistent
with applicable tipping-off restrictions.

## Recordkeeping

All escalations, investigation notes, and filed reports (or documented
decisions not to file) are retained per the Recordkeeping & Retention
Policy.
