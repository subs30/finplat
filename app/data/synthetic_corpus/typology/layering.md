# Typology: Layering

**Document type:** Typology
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — illustrative only, not real compliance guidance.

## Description

Layering is the second stage of the classic three-stage money laundering
model (placement, layering, integration). Its purpose is to obscure the
origin of funds by moving them through multiple transactions, accounts,
instruments, or jurisdictions, creating distance between the money's
illicit source and its eventual, seemingly legitimate destination.
Layering does not itself introduce funds into the financial system
(placement does) or make them available for ordinary use (integration
does) — it exists purely to make the money's origin harder to trace.

## Common Patterns

- Funds moved through a chain of intermediary accounts in quick
  succession, often with little or no economic rationale for each hop.
- Conversion between instruments — cash to wire, wire to cryptocurrency,
  cryptocurrency to a different fiat currency — purely to break the audit
  trail rather than for any transactional purpose.
- Routing through multiple jurisdictions, particularly ones with weaker
  transaction-history transparency, before funds arrive at their final
  destination.
- Use of shell or shelf companies as intermediate holders of funds with
  no genuine business operations (see the Shell Company Red Flags
  typology).
- Round-number transfers or transfers that mirror each other in timing
  and amount across a chain of accounts, suggesting the same funds moving
  through rather than independent economic activity.

## Red Flags

- An account that receives a transfer and forwards a similar amount
  onward within a very short window, with no apparent business reason to
  hold or transform the funds in between.
- A chain of transfers across multiple accounts/entities that, when
  traced, all connect back to the same beneficial owner or a small,
  tightly connected group.
- Transactions that are economically irrational on their own (e.g., fees
  or currency conversion costs that make no sense for a legitimate
  purpose) but make sense as a way to break a paper trail.

## Investigative Approach

Layering is best identified by tracing funds across hops rather than
looking at any single account in isolation — an investigator should
follow the chain forward and backward from a flagged transaction,
looking for the pattern of rapid pass-through and jurisdictional or
instrument switching described above.
