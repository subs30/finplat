# Case Write-up: CASE-2025-0402 — Multi-City Cash Funnel Account

**Document type:** Case write-up
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — fictional case, not a real investigation.

## Summary

A geographic-anomaly alert flagged a personal savings account receiving
cash deposits from branches in seven different cities across three
states over a two-month period, despite the account holder's address on
file being a single mid-sized city.

## Observed Activity

The account received 22 cash deposits ranging from $400 to $1,900 across
the review period, made at branches in the account holder's home city as
well as six other cities the holder had no documented connection to (no
listed employer, family, or prior address in any of them). Roughly every
7-10 days, once the balance crossed approximately $8,000, a wire transfer
of $7,500-$8,500 was sent to the same recipient account at another
institution, after which the deposit-and-transfer cycle repeated.

## Analysis

The geographic dispersion of deposit locations relative to the account
holder's documented footprint, combined with the repeating
consolidate-then-transfer cycle, matches the funnel account typology
closely. The account holder's stated occupation (part-time retail
employee) and declared income were inconsistent with cash deposit volume
of this scale and geographic spread. When contacted, the account holder
stated they had been asked by an acquaintance to "help move money for a
small fee" and deposit cash "wherever was convenient," without further
detail about the acquaintance's identity or business.

The consistent recipient of the outbound wires — the same account at
another institution across every cycle — was the strongest single
finding, since it demonstrated the dispersed deposits were converging on
one destination rather than being independent, unrelated activity.

## Disposition

The case was escalated and a report was filed. The account was
restricted pending further review, and the information about the
consistent outbound recipient was shared with the receiving institution's
compliance contact, consistent with the organization's information-
sharing practices for active investigations.

## Lessons Noted

Geographic deposit mapping against the account holder's known footprint
was the fastest path to identifying this pattern — the individual
transaction amounts were unremarkable on their own and would not have
triggered attention without the location-dispersion signal.
