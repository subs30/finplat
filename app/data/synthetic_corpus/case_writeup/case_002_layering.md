# Case Write-up: CASE-2025-0189 — Multi-Hop Wire Layering

**Document type:** Case write-up
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — fictional case, not a real investigation.

## Summary

This case began with an alert on an incoming wire of $180,000 into a
business account for a consulting entity incorporated eight months
earlier, followed by an outbound wire of $175,000 two business days
later to an account at a different institution.

## Observed Activity

Tracing the destination account (obtained via information sharing with
the receiving institution's compliance team) showed the funds were
forwarded again within 48 hours, minus a small retained amount, to a
third account held by yet another recently formed entity in a different
jurisdiction. That third account, in turn, forwarded the bulk of the
funds to a fourth account roughly one week later. At each hop, the
receiving entity had no observable business activity, website, or
operating history consistent with a genuine consulting relationship, and
each hop retained only a small percentage of the incoming amount.

## Analysis

The chain — rapid pass-through, minimal retained balance at each stage,
newly formed entities with no operating footprint, and jurisdictional
hopping — matches the layering typology closely: no single hop had an
apparent independent business rationale, and the pattern's only
consistent feature was moving the bulk of the funds onward quickly. The
consulting entity that received the original inbound wire could not
produce a contract, invoice, or other documentation supporting a genuine
consulting engagement of this size when asked.

## Disposition

Given the multi-hop pattern and lack of business rationale at every
stage, the case was escalated and a report was filed. The relationship
with the consulting entity was subsequently exited following the account
closure procedure, since enhanced due diligence could not establish a
credible explanation for the activity.

## Lessons Noted

This case relied heavily on cross-institution information sharing to
trace beyond the first hop — without visibility into the second and
third accounts, the pattern would have looked like a single unremarkable
outbound wire.
