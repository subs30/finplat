# Case Write-up: CASE-2025-0114 — Retail Deposit Structuring

**Document type:** Case write-up
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — fictional case, not a real investigation.

## Summary

This case was opened after transaction monitoring flagged a personal
checking account with an unusual pattern of cash deposits over a
three-week period. The account holder is a sole proprietor operating a
small retail business, with a stated average monthly deposit history of
roughly $6,000 prior to the flagged period.

## Observed Activity

Between the 2nd and the 21st of the review month, the account received
14 separate cash deposits ranging from $2,800 to $2,950 each, made across
six different branch locations. No single deposit reached the
institution's $3,000 reporting threshold. Deposits were frequently made
on the same day at different branches, and on three occasions two
deposits were made within an hour of each other at branches roughly 20
minutes apart.

## Analysis

The clustering of deposit amounts just under the reporting threshold,
combined with the use of multiple branches and same-day multi-branch
deposits, is consistent with the structuring/smurfing typology: a
deliberate pattern to avoid triggering reporting thresholds rather than
an incidental byproduct of normal business cash handling. The account
holder's stated retail business does not obviously explain cash receipts
of this volume or this multi-branch pattern — most small retail cash
receipts would be deposited at a single, convenient branch near the
business location.

When asked to explain the pattern during a routine account review call,
the account holder described "helping out a friend's business with cash
deposits" without further detail, and could not identify the friend's
business by name.

## Disposition

The case was escalated to the SAR review process per the Suspicious
Activity Reporting Policy. A report was filed, and the account was placed
under enhanced monitoring pending the outcome of the report. No further
structuring-pattern deposits were observed in the 60 days following
escalation.

## Lessons Noted

The multi-branch, same-day deposit pattern was the strongest signal here
— threshold-adjacent amounts alone, without the branch-dispersion
pattern, would have been a weaker indicator on their own.
