# Case Write-up: CASE-2025-0355 — Unresolvable Beneficial Ownership Chain

**Document type:** Case write-up
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — fictional case, not a real investigation.

## Summary

During a periodic high-risk customer review, enhanced due diligence on a
corporate customer revealed an ownership structure that could not be
resolved to a natural person after standard verification steps, despite
the customer having operated the account for over a year with no prior
concerns.

## Observed Activity

The customer, a holding entity, listed its sole shareholder as a second
entity registered in a different jurisdiction. That second entity's
filings listed its own shareholder as a third entity in a third
jurisdiction, whose filings in turn listed a fourth entity as the
majority shareholder. At each layer, the registered address matched an
address shared by a very large number of unrelated companies, consistent
with a company-formation-agent address rather than a genuine operating
location. Attempts to obtain further beneficial ownership documentation
directly from the customer were met with generic corporate literature
that did not identify any natural person.

Transaction activity on the account, reviewed as part of the same EDD
cycle, showed a pattern of receiving large incoming transfers and
forwarding the bulk onward within days, with minimal retained balance —
consistent with a pass-through role rather than genuine operating
activity for the "trading" business described on file.

## Analysis

This matches the shell company typology: a multi-jurisdictional chain of
entities sharing formation-agent addresses, no resolvable natural-person
owner, and account activity consistent with a pass-through role rather
than a genuinely operating trading business. The combination of an
unresolvable ownership chain and pass-through transaction behavior was
treated as mutually reinforcing evidence, rather than either alone being
treated as decisive.

## Disposition

Because the beneficial ownership chain could not be resolved despite
reasonable diligence, and the observed transaction pattern was
inconsistent with the stated business purpose, the relationship was
escalated for an exit decision under the EDD Policy's provisions for
relationships where EDD reveals activity inconsistent with stated
purpose. The account was restricted while the exit was processed, and a
report was filed.

## Lessons Noted

The account had no prior escalations and had operated for over a year —
this case is a reminder that periodic EDD review, not just event-driven
triggers, surfaced this finding, since nothing about the account's
day-to-day activity alone had triggered an earlier alert.
