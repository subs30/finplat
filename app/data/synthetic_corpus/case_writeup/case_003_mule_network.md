# Case Write-up: CASE-2025-0233 — Recruited Mule Account Cluster

**Document type:** Case write-up
**Status:** Synthetic placeholder — fictional case, not a real investigation.

## Summary

A fraud-proceeds investigation identified a cluster of five personal
accounts, opened by different individuals with no apparent relationship
to each other, that each began receiving multiple incoming transfers from
victims of a reported romance scam shortly after account opening.

## Observed Activity

Each of the five accounts followed a similar pattern: opened within the
prior four months, dormant or near-dormant until a trigger date, then
receiving between four and nine incoming transfers from individuals later
identified as scam victims, ranging from $500 to $4,000 per transfer.
Within 24-48 hours of each incoming transfer, the receiving account
forwarded the bulk of the funds (retaining roughly 5-10%) to one of two
consolidation accounts, both held by the same corporate entity.

Interviews with two of the five account holders (conducted by law
enforcement, summarized here with permission) indicated both had
responded to a social-media job posting offering payment for "processing
online payments," and had been instructed by the poster to forward
incoming funds to a provided account number, keeping a small percentage
as a fee.

## Analysis

This matches the mule account typology closely: legitimate individuals,
recruited under a pretext, whose accounts exhibit the pass-through
signature (funds in, funds out quickly, small retained residual) with no
plausible independent business relationship to the many unrelated
senders. The consolidation of five separate mule accounts into two shared
destination accounts was the key structural finding connecting an
otherwise dispersed set of individual victim complaints into a single
network.

## Disposition

Reports were filed on all five mule accounts and on the two consolidation
accounts. All seven accounts were restricted pending further law
enforcement inquiry. The five individual account holders were referred
for victim-support resources rather than treated as principals, based on
the evidence that they were themselves recruited under false pretenses.

## Lessons Noted

The individual accounts, viewed alone, each looked like a modest fraud
case. It was only the shared destination-account link across all five
that revealed the network structure.
